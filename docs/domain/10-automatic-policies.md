## Part 10 — Automatic and Operational Policies

### 10.1 Surplus eligibility and prohibited offers

Every publication requires a reason explaining **why this stock is surplus now**. Permitted classifications are daily excess, near expiry, minor disclosed damage, damaged packaging, remaining lot, construction leftover, offcut, excess production, discontinued/commercially displaced stock, intended-disposal material, free surplus, or reusable material. Multiple reasons may be declared; one primary reason is sufficient for simple publication.

Evidence is proportionate: a plain declaration normally suffices for a low-risk local offer; repetitive ordinary inventory, improbable savings, or suspicious condition claims require clarification. Free status and recurring availability are not proof of wrongdoing, but neither proves surplus. An eligible discontinued retail item is allowed if its displaced-stock circumstance is genuine.

Prohibited: ordinary resale without a surplus circumstance; generic personal used-item listings; fabricated inventory; counterfeits; stolen goods; illegal goods; misleading listings; unsafe goods; expired food; recalled products; compromised food-contact packaging; weapons; controlled substances; hazardous chemicals or waste; medicines; live animals; sensitive personal documents; and items requiring specialist safety assessment absent such qualification. These are proposed platform exclusions, not a statement of jurisdiction-specific law. Used reusable business materials such as pallets can qualify when safe provenance and an actual surplus circumstance are declared.

Ineligible ordinary retail gets an explanation and publication refusal/removal. A first honest classification mistake normally receives correction guidance. Fraudulent classification receives a case and proportionate sanction.

### 10.2 Initial category taxonomy

One primary category and an optional simple subcategory are enough:

- **Food:** bakery, prepared meals, packaged food. Require preparation/age context, relevant expiry, storage, condition, and known dietary safety disclosures.
- **Fruit and Vegetables:** produce and mixed baskets. Disclose ripeness, cosmetic damage, edible condition, and quantity/weight basis.
- **Construction:** tiles, blocks, flooring, pipes, remaining materials. Disclose dimensions, condition, packaging, and safe handling; uncertain structural suitability cannot be marketed as certified.
- **Wood and Carpentry:** wood, MDF, offcuts. Disclose useful dimensions or assortment basis, treatment/contamination if known, and handling risks.
- **Paper and Printing:** offcuts, excess paper, printing leftovers. Exclude confidential material; disclose sizes and usability.
- **Packaging:** boxes, pallets, containers. Disclose previous use and contamination; no unsupported food-contact claim.
- **Agriculture:** eligible non-hazardous farm surplus. Disclose condition, intended use, and relevant handling; restricted products stay excluded.
- **Workshop and Parts:** identifiable safe excess parts and materials. Disclose compatibility, wear/damage, and provenance; unsafe safety-critical components are excluded.
- **Textiles and Offcuts:** fabrics and remnants. Disclose material where known, dimensions/assortment, and condition.
- **Other Reusable Materials:** safe eligible surplus outside the above. Repeated patterns may justify a future category; this category is not an exception to prohibited goods.

### 10.3 Food and surprise-bag policy

Food must distinguish prepared today, prepared previously, near expiry, surprise assortment, and free food. “Yesterday's product” is a factual age disclosure, not automatic proof of safety. Record relevant preparation date/time or stated age, expiry when applicable, declared condition, refrigeration or temperature-sensitive handling needs, hard pickup limit, known allergens/material dietary risks, and the establishment's condition declaration.

Supplier statements are displayed as declarations, not platform certifications. No claim that a surprise bag is safe for a dietary restriction may be made without a reliable contents basis. A surprise bag still requires a general assortment, promised minimum quantity or substantiated ordinary-value basis, condition/age information, and available safety disclosures. If essential safety information cannot be given for an unknown assortment, do not publish it.

Damaged outer packaging is eligible only when product integrity is unaffected and damage is disclosed. Opened or compromised protective food packaging, expired food, known spoilage, or doubtful safe handling prevents publication and pickup. Grace, free status, discounts, and consumer consent cannot override a hard safety limit. Food donations obey the same policy. A new publication cannot reset the physical expiry of the same stock.

### 10.4 Booking, release, and closure

Booking opening must precede booking closing. Closing must occur no later than the pickup end or hard safety limit. Pickup opening must precede pickup end. A scheduled or current offer can have pickup opening later than booking opening, provided the later promised interval is realistic and public.

At any failed/cancelled commitment, remaining quantity goes to:

1. **Available** if the offer is Published, booking remains open, supplier is eligible, units still physically exist, and safe new booking is possible.
2. **Available but undiscoverable** if temporarily Paused and those physical/temporal conditions otherwise remain valid; reopening is needed for a new booking.
3. **Expired** if booking time has ended with no explicit earlier withdrawal reason.
4. **Withdrawn** if supplier cancellation, shortage, safety, removal, or voluntary closure makes resale ineligible.

This precedence prevents unsafe stock from returning because a consumer cancelled. A terminal offer never regains available stock automatically. Confirmed reserved stock persists after booking expiry only until its accepted pickup outcome. A transaction becomes operationally settled when all accepted units have a disposition; it becomes financially settled only when payment, refunds, and disputes are resolved.

### 10.5 Progressive price rules

Before publication, the supplier can choose a fixed price or a valid schedule. Example: 18:00 at BRL 10.00, 19:00 at BRL 5.00, 20:00 at BRL 0.00, with booking closing later than 20:00. The greatest stage timestamp less than or equal to current time determines the current price. At the exact 19:00 boundary, a newly accepted commitment uses BRL 5.00.

An accepted 18:59 commitment at BRL 10.00 remains BRL 10.00 while paying at 19:01 within its deadline. A consumer merely looking at BRL 10.00 at 18:59 and requesting at 19:01 is asked to accept BRL 5.00. A price change during an unaccepted request requires explicit acceptance of the resulting price; a price change after accepted allocation does not.

Future stages may be altered while the offer is non-terminal. Past stages remain history. During an active schedule, replacement future stages cannot increase above the current scheduled price. Cancelling the schedule freezes the current price; any later manual price increase for uncommitted stock is a separately declared prospective change. Zero cannot be followed by a positive stage in the same schedule. A paused offer evaluates the current effective stage when resumed; it does not resume an obsolete higher price.

Making an offer free changes payment mode only for new commitments. Existing payment-pending consumers may cancel and request a new free reservation if quantity and caps allow; they have no guaranteed priority over others. Existing paid consumers are not retroactively refunded solely for a later discount. A new free reservation is genuinely zero cost, including all compulsory charges.

### 10.6 Alteration policy

Before any accepted commitment, the supplier may change title, description, category, condition, format, unit definition, quantity, price, point, booking/pickup intervals, and schedule, provided publication validity is rechecked. A unit-format change requires a complete new quantity declaration so counts remain meaningful.

After any commitment, including an Awaiting Payment commitment:

- Correct non-material typographical errors and add truthful clarifications, with history.
- Change price or future price stages for uncommitted units only.
- Add genuine units of the identical sellable-unit definition.
- Withdraw only available units, explicitly. A physical shortage of reserved stock requires named cancellation and refunds.
- Extend booking within unchanged promised pickup and safety limits; shorten booking only for new commitments and preserve live payment deadlines. If that preservation is impossible, use explicit cancellation with refund consequences rather than silent shortening.
- Keep supplier, establishment, pickup point, category defining the goods, unit definition, minimum contents, and material condition fixed. Materially different surplus requires a new offer.
- Do not shorten a protected pickup interval. A supplier-proposed extension may be accepted explicitly by an individual consumer, within verified service and safety limits; refusal leaves original terms intact. No automatic extension exists in the MVP.
- Record newly discovered safety facts immediately and cancel affected entitlements when necessary. Immutability does not mean concealing danger.

A changed pickup location, materially different product, or worse condition cannot be imposed. The simplest remedy is cancellation/refund and an optional fresh offer. A consumer may reject any undisclosed material substitution without penalty.

### 10.7 Pickup exceptions

The consumer receives a verifiable reservation confirmation usable by an authorized collector. A supplier must verify the entitlement without requesting unnecessary account or financial details. A public offer reference alone is insufficient.

Early pickup is refused unless inside the accepted interval. Late pickup within the published effective grace is allowed only if the supplier remains open and goods remain safe. Beyond that deadline, no entitlement remains; a fresh accepted reservation is required if a new eligible offer exists. No operator can reset an unsafe item's deadline.

A third-party collector must be nominated by the consumer before verification. The nomination may be replaced before handover, with history; only one final handover is permitted. Unnominated collectors cannot obtain goods merely by knowing a public reference. Exceptional identity verification must protect privacy and retain sufficient evidence of authority.

If the establishment is closed, goods are missing, the item materially differs, or safety is doubtful, record failed fulfillment, end affected entitlement, and refund uncollected paid quantity. Do not attribute no-show. An agreed separable-unit partial handover is final: the received portion is completed, the remainder is cancelled, and the original per-unit price allocates the refund. A lot or bag cannot be partially fulfilled as a smaller version of the promised unit.

### 10.8 Free-offer fairness

The first-time consumer is eligible without a minimum score. Apply the two-live-free-reservation cap, offer quantity cap, short but usable pickup interval, and ordinary no-show attribution. Count linked accounts together only after corroborated abuse review; a shared home or pickup point alone is insufficient evidence.

Restrictions for resolved recurring no-shows are proportionate under Part 12. Future scarce donation programs may require objective eligibility published in advance; they must not fabricate a reputation requirement that excludes every new user. Free surplus has no hidden handling, reservation, or pickup fee.

### 10.9 Alert relevance, location, and privacy

An alert is eligible only if the consumer opted in, the offer is currently discoverable, the pickup point falls inside the selected radius, category and price/free filters match, and enough pickup time remains. A followed establishment may prioritize ranking but does not override explicit filters. Approximate distance is straight-line distance between the declared discovery areas/point and is labeled approximate.

The default cap is one new-offer alert per consumer per offer and up to three opportunity alerts per day. Price-drop alerts are a later optional preference and require a materially lower stage, renewed relevance, and the same daily cap. Repeated publication of substantially the same stock cannot evade the cap. Reservation deadline and cancellation notices are operational notices tied to an obligation, with minimum necessary content.

Consumer precise location is not shown to suppliers or the public. Public establishment addresses may be shown for discovery. Future residential pickup shows approximate area before a confirmed reservation and exact address afterward to its authorized holder; warnings should disclose that exact location is released only on confirmation. Ending a reservation does not require indefinite access to a private address.

### 10.10 Future demand and recurrence policies

Wanted-surplus requests have Draft, Active, Fulfilled, Expired, and Cancelled states. Publishing validates category, quantity/unit basis, radius, and a future validity limit. A positive declared pickup reduces remaining demand only after consumer acknowledgment. Offers match category, usable condition, comparable quantity basis, radius, price, and overlapping validity; no match creates stock priority. Suppliers see aggregated demand or a consented non-sensitive request, never a consumer's private home location.

Recurrence templates have Draft, Active, Paused, and Retired states. Each weekday occurrence asks the supplier to confirm actual quantity, current condition, and service interval. Missed confirmation, zero surplus, holiday closure, expired booking window, or missing allowance means no publication. Each confirmed occurrence consumes one publication allowance and owns its own commitments. Pausing/retiring a template stops future preparation; existing occurrences remain accountable.
